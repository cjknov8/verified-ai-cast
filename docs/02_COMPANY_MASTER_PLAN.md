# Company Master Plan

## Mission

Create the public trust infrastructure that proves when digital entertainment uses have been officially authorized by the relevant rights holder.

## Category

The category is **digital rights verification infrastructure**, not AI generation, talent assets, or creative tooling.

The company coordinates four kinds of evidence:

1. Identity: the person or organization is who it claims to be.
2. Authority: the approver has authority over the relevant rights.
3. Authorization: a specific use was approved under defined terms.
4. Integrity: the published content and location correspond to the approved record.

## Strategic Wedge

Performers are the correct initial category because:

- The harm is intuitive and visible.
- Consent, control, compensation, and transparency are already industry priorities.
- Approval can be scoped to a finished result.
- Agencies provide concentrated distribution and repeat usage.
- Public certificates create an immediate verification object.

The wedge should be described as official appearance approval, not likeness rental.

## Five-Phase Evolution

### Phase 1: Verified AI Cast

Users: actors, models, influencers, voice actors, small agencies, AI filmmakers.

Proof to establish:

- Rights-holder verification can be operationalized.
- Finished results can be reviewed efficiently.
- Public certificates reduce buyer and reputational risk.
- Revocation and URL checks are understood.

Exit criteria:

- Repeat agency usage
- Creators voluntarily link certificates
- Public verification traffic
- Low dispute rate
- Reliable approval turnaround

### Phase 2: Verified Productions

Add verified creator, studio, director, production company, and project identities. A production record becomes the container for certificates, contributors, disclosures, rights claims, and release history.

The product moves from "this actor approved this appearance" to "these parties and rights records support this production."

### Phase 3: Verified Brand Placements

Add brand rights holders, product families, territories, category conflicts, placement approvals, and brand guideline evidence.

The platform should first support controlled, premium placement programs rather than an open brand catalog.

### Phase 4: Verified Rights Registry

Generalize the registry to performers, brands, characters, game IP, entertainment IP, product designs, and other digital assets.

At this stage the public identity should no longer be actor-specific.

### Phase 5: AI Entertainment Trust Infrastructure

Offer:

- Public registry and lookup
- Verification API and webhooks
- Enterprise issuance and policy tools
- Interoperable credentials
- Transparency log
- Trust lists and issuer governance
- Platform integrations
- Independent assurance and audit reporting

## Strategic Positioning

The company should be:

- Like IMDb in discoverability and industry reference value
- Like DocuSign in recorded intent and agreement evidence
- Like Creative Commons in understandable rights vocabulary
- Like Stripe in infrastructure and developer integration
- Like Bloomberg or LexisNexis in professional information value
- Like a certificate authority in issuance, status, and revocation discipline

It should not claim to be legally equivalent to a government property registry or accredited web PKI until governance and law support that claim.

## What the Company Does Not Do

- Guarantee that unauthorized copying stops
- Determine all underlying legal rights without evidence and counsel
- Replace contracts, unions, courts, or government records
- Certify that content is true
- Certify that an AI model was ethically trained
- Host an open exchange for raw identity models
- Treat provenance metadata as proof of legal permission

## Company Moat

Features are copyable. The defensible asset is the accepted body of records and relationships:

- Verified rights-holder identities
- Authority and representation history
- Standardized approval vocabulary
- Certificate and revocation history
- Public relying-party traffic
- Agency and studio workflow integration
- Platform verification integrations
- Dispute-handling credibility
- Longitudinal audit evidence
- Brand and union participation

## Trust Flywheel

1. Rights holders join to control official authorization.
2. Creators seek certificates to gain legitimacy and distribution access.
3. Platforms and advertisers check certificates to reduce risk.
4. Verification demand makes certification more valuable.
5. More certified projects produce richer histories and standards.
6. Better histories reduce onboarding and underwriting cost.
7. Stronger relying-party adoption makes the registry the expected venue.

## Strategic Risks

| Risk | Response |
| --- | --- |
| Seen as an AI marketplace | No raw assets; lead with registry and approval records |
| Rights holder distrust | Independent controls, limited licenses, revocation, clear data boundaries |
| False authority claims | Multi-source verification and authority expiration |
| Certificate abuse | URL, content, status, and signature validation |
| Platform self-preferencing | Open verification standard and neutral governance |
| Incumbent copying | Build accepted records, relationships, and relying-party integrations |
| Legal fragmentation | Jurisdiction profiles and scope-specific contract templates |
| Luxury dilution | Private programs, selective issuance, no mass discounting |
| Overclaiming legitimacy | Publish assurance level and precise certificate meaning |

## 24-Month Strategic Priorities

### 0-6 months

- Validate performer and agency onboarding.
- Define identity and authority assurance levels.
- Make agreements, approvals, certificates, and revocation persistent.
- Publish certificate semantics and relying-party policy.
- Run 10-20 controlled certified releases.

### 6-12 months

- Add domain verification, media hashes, evidence bundles, API lookup, and monitoring.
- Establish a rights-holder advisory council.
- Sign small agency and independent studio programs.
- Publish transparency and dispute reports.

### 12-18 months

- Launch verified production profiles.
- Add enterprise issuance and verification integrations.
- Pilot one selective brand placement program.
- Begin independent security and controls review.

### 18-24 months

- Introduce the broader registry parent brand.
- Add cross-category rights assets and authority grants.
- Publish an open certificate schema.
- Pursue platform, advertiser, and industry-association reliance.

## North Star and Metrics

North star:

> Number of active, externally relied-upon rights certificates with verified authority, content binding, and distribution binding.

Supporting metrics:

- Verified rights holders and represented rights assets
- Certificates issued, renewed, expired, revoked, and disputed
- Percentage with verified domains and content hashes
- External verification requests per certificate
- Creator repeat rate
- Agency roster penetration
- Median review and revocation propagation time
- Certificate mismatch detection rate
- Dispute resolution time
- Revenue by workflow, registry, and enterprise infrastructure
