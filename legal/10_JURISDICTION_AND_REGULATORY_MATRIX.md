# Jurisdiction and Regulatory Matrix

> Research snapshot dated 2026-06-12. Not legal advice. Laws, effective dates, implementing rules, and territorial application must be confirmed by qualified counsel before launch or expansion.

## Cross-Jurisdiction Product Rule

The certificate should record authorization facts but never state that all legal requirements are satisfied. Launch reviews must separately cover publicity/personality rights, privacy and biometrics, copyright, trademark, advertising, labor, consumer protection, electronic signatures, platform liability, and AI disclosure.

## United States

### Federal

Key issues:

- No single comprehensive federal right of publicity currently replaces state law.
- Copyright generally does not itself provide a complete remedy for a person's replicated identity.
- FTC law may apply to deceptive endorsements, impersonation, and unfair practices.
- The federal NO FAKES Act of 2025, S.1367, remained at introduced status on the latest Congress.gov action shown as April 9, 2025. It must not be treated as enacted law without a new status check.
- Electronic signatures may implicate the federal E-SIGN Act and state UETA variants.
- Labor and collective bargaining terms may impose consent and compensation rules beyond general law.

Product implications:

- state-by-state rights profile
- explicit written consent
- deceptive endorsement controls
- union and contract flags
- federal bill monitoring
- no assertion that copyright ownership equals digital-replica authority

### California

California AB 2602 added Labor Code section 927. For specified personal or professional services agreements, certain digital-replica provisions concerning new performances fixed on or after January 1, 2025 are unenforceable when intended uses are not reasonably specific and the individual lacked qualifying counsel or union representation.

Product implications:

- reasonably specific use descriptions
- capture representation by counsel or union
- clear and conspicuous commercial terms
- separate consent and scope records
- preserve the contract version and signer evidence
- review California postmortem and publicity rules separately

### New York

New York Civil Rights Law sections 50 and 51 require written consent for specified advertising or trade uses of a living person's name, portrait, picture, likeness, or voice and provide injunctive and damages remedies.

Section 50-f addresses postmortem rights and unauthorized digital replicas of deceased performers, includes successor and licensee concepts, and provides a public registration mechanism for certain successor claims. The current statute text should be checked for the exact effective revision applicable to each use.

Product implications:

- written consent
- living and deceased rights-holder workflows
- successor-in-interest verification
- state registry evidence link
- exceptions and expressive-use review
- New York-specific advertising disclosure review for synthetic performers

### SAG-AFTRA and Industry Agreements

Consent, compensation, control, disclosure, and defined digital-replica use are recurring requirements in SAG-AFTRA AI materials and negotiated agreements. Contract coverage must be captured per production rather than treated as a user-profile attribute.

Product implications:

- union status and agreement version
- covered work and bargaining agreement
- performer-specific consent
- compensation and usage reporting
- no waiver of collective bargaining protections

## European Union

### EU AI Act

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 entered into force on August 1, 2024. European Commission guidance states that transparency rules, including labeling of certain deepfakes and identification of AI-generated content, apply from August 2026. Article 50 and implementing guidance require project-specific counsel review, including exceptions and the roles of provider and deployer.

Product implications:

- separate rights-holder approval from statutory AI disclosure
- machine-readable and visible disclosure fields
- identify provider, deployer, and publication context
- preserve disclosure evidence
- monitor final guidance and codes of practice

### GDPR and Personality Rights

EU deployment also requires analysis of:

- GDPR lawful basis, transparency, minimization, security, retention, and data subject rights
- biometric data when used for unique identification
- international transfers
- national personality, image, voice, moral, and publicity-like rights
- consumer protection and misleading commercial practices
- copyright and performers' rights

Product implications:

- EU data processing map and DPA
- avoid unnecessary biometric identification
- region-specific retention
- data protection impact assessment for high-risk processing
- member-state legal profiles

## South Korea

### Publicity-Like and Unfair Competition Protection

The Unfair Competition Prevention and Trade Secret Protection Act includes protection relevant to unauthorized commercial use of a person's widely recognized name, portrait, voice, signature, and other identifying signs with economic value. Exact elements, remedies, and current numbering require Korean counsel review.

Product implications:

- identity and economic-value evidence
- explicit commercial scope
- Korean-language consent
- performer, agency, and advertiser responsibility mapping
- misuse and takedown records

### Portrait, Privacy, and Personal Information

Korean portrait and personality interests arise across constitutional, civil, privacy, and unfair competition doctrines. The Personal Information Protection Act may apply to identity documents, face and voice materials, account data, and cross-border processing.

Product implications:

- separate consent for verification and licensing
- sensitive source-material controls
- Korean retention and cross-border notices
- local representative or regulatory requirements as applicable
- cautious use of face matching and liveness vendors

### Copyright and Performers' Rights

Copyright in source media, final productions, music, scripts, and recorded performances remains separate from portrait, voice, trademark, and contractual rights. A creator must provide chain-of-title evidence.

### AI Basic Act

South Korea's Framework Act on the Development of Artificial Intelligence and Establishment of Trust Foundation and implementing requirements should be reviewed for the exact obligations effective in 2026, including transparency for synthetic outputs that may be mistaken for real content. The product should not rely on secondary summaries for launch compliance.

Product implications:

- Korean AI disclosure profile
- visible or machine-readable marking requirements as applicable
- provider/deployer role analysis
- maintain current official-law and decree references

## Jurisdiction Matrix

| Issue | US | EU | South Korea |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Publicity/personality | Primarily state law plus contracts | Member-state personality/image rights | Civil, constitutional, privacy, and unfair competition doctrines |
| Digital replica | State laws, contracts, proposed federal law | AI transparency plus national rights | Existing rights plus AI framework and sector rules |
| Written consent | Often critical; expressly required in some states | Evidence of lawful authorization and contract | Strongly recommended; context-specific legal requirements |
| AI disclosure | Federal/state/sector and platform specific | AI Act transparency from August 2026, subject to scope | AI Basic Act and implementing rules require current review |
| Privacy | Sectoral/state patchwork | GDPR | PIPA |
| Biometrics | State-specific plus sector rules | Special-category restrictions when used for unique identification | PIPA and guidance |
| Postmortem | State-specific | Member-state-specific | Estate, contract, and applicable personality-right analysis |
| E-signature | E-SIGN/UETA framework | eIDAS and national law | Electronic signature and contract law |
| Platform liability | Federal and state frameworks | DSA and national law may apply | Platform and intermediary rules require local review |

## Launch Gate

Before accepting a live certificate in a jurisdiction, counsel should approve:

- certificate claim wording
- consent and authority evidence
- required disclosures
- privacy and retention profile
- takedown and dispute process
- governing agreement variant
- public data fields
- regulatory monitoring owner
