# Business Model, Go-to-Market, and Network Effects

## Revenue Principle

Charge for reducing authorization risk and operating trusted records. Do not make revenue depend primarily on maximizing identity-asset transactions.

## Revenue Portfolio

| Revenue line | Buyer | Strategic role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rights-holder or agency subscription | Agencies, estates, brands | Policy, roster, authority, approvals, monitoring |
| Creator review fee | Creators and studios | Pays for review operations regardless of approval |
| Certificate issuance fee | Project buyer | Covers issuance and evidence processing |
| License transaction fee | Buyer and/or rights holder | Optional commerce support |
| Registry monitoring | Rights holders, brands, platforms | URL, account, content, expiration, and misuse monitoring |
| Enterprise program | Studios, advertisers, platforms | SSO, APIs, bulk workflows, audit exports, SLAs |
| Verification API | Platforms and ad-tech | High-volume status and match checks |
| Rights management services | Rights holders | Managed onboarding, authority cleanup, policy operations |
| Enhanced due diligence | Enterprises | Higher-assurance identity and authority review |
| Audit and evidence export | Legal and compliance teams | Matter-specific evidence bundles |

Public certificate lookup should remain free. Charging the public to verify would weaken the trust network.

## Pricing Architecture

Use three independent price dimensions:

1. **Workflow price**: review labor, turnaround, complexity, deliverables.
2. **Rights price**: commercial scope negotiated for the rights holder.
3. **Trust infrastructure price**: certificate, monitoring, registry, and API.

This separation prevents the platform from appearing to set the market value of a person's identity.

Value:

### Demand-side beachhead

## Go-to-Market Sequence

### Step 1: Curated Performer Program

Recruit a small cohort with real audience or professional credits. Onboarding should be white-glove and free or subsidized. Create high-quality policy profiles and require no raw model upload.

### Step 2: Certified Release Pilots

Select projects already likely to be approved. Produce case studies showing review, conditions, public verification, and distribution acceptance.

### Step 3: Boutique Agency Program

Offer roster onboarding, reviewer seats, reusable policies, project intake, certificate management, and reporting.

### Step 4: Creator and Studio Workflow

Let verified creators reuse organization identity, production records, insurance/compliance evidence, and payment details.

### Step 5: Relying-Party Adoption

Target advertisers, festivals, distributors, platforms, insurers, and completion-bond or legal teams that benefit from checking authorization.

### Step 6: Verified Productions

Aggregate performer certificates and other rights records under a verified production page.

### Step 7: Selective Brand Pilots

Launch controlled brand placement programs only after the platform can demonstrate premium curation and reliable enforcement.

## Adoption Messaging

For rights holders:

> Define what official use looks like, review finished work, and preserve a public record.

For creators:

> Turn permission into proof that partners and audiences can verify.

For agencies:

> Operate digital replica approvals with policies, authority records, and audit history.

For advertisers and platforms:

> Check whether the claimed approval is active, scoped, and connected to the content and location.

## Network Effects

### Rights-Holder Network Effect

More authoritative profiles make the registry a more complete place to seek legitimate approvals.

### Creator Network Effect

More registered rights holders increase the number of projects that can be certified, attracting more creators.

### Relying-Party Network Effect

More advertisers and platforms checking certificates increases the value of holding one.

### Data and History Effect

Every approval, expiration, and revocation improves policy templates, risk detection, and institutional memory.

### Standards Effect

Once certificate IDs, statuses, and schemas are embedded in contracts and platform workflows, switching costs become organizational rather than technical.

### Reputation Effect

Consistent decisions and transparent dispute handling create a brand of reliability that cannot be copied by shipping the same screens.

## If OpenAI, Google, or Adobe Copies Every Feature

They can copy:

- submission forms
- certificate pages
- hashes and watermarks
- approval workflows
- APIs

They cannot instantly copy:

- verified authority relationships
- historical approvals and revocations
- neutral acceptance across competing generation platforms
- agency and rights-holder trust
- relying-party integration and policy references
- a reputation for independent adjudication

The defense is neutrality. A registry owned by one generation vendor may be viewed as certifying its own ecosystem. Verified Rights Registry should accept content from any tool and separate generation provenance from rights authorization.

## Why Each Participant Joins

- **Actors**: control official association and preserve negotiating leverage.
- **Agencies**: standardize requests, reduce review chaos, and demonstrate authority.
- **Creators**: obtain promotable legitimacy and lower distribution friction.
- **Studios**: consolidate rights evidence and avoid repeated due diligence.
- **Brands**: distinguish approved placement from imitation and protect prestige.
- **Advertisers**: reduce endorsement, disclosure, and reputational risk.
- **Platforms**: gain a structured signal for labels, disputes, and monetization decisions.

## Moat Metrics

- Share of certificates with external verification traffic
- Number of contracts that require registry IDs
- Number of platforms consuming status webhooks
- Percentage of rights-holder profiles with renewed authority verification
- Historical depth per rights asset
- Repeat projects across multiple counterparties
- Median time to resolve a competing claim
- Brand and agency program retention

## Business Risks

- Taking a high percentage of license value can create marketplace distrust.
- Advertising revenue tied to certificate pages can compromise neutrality.
- Pay-to-verify branding can create a two-tier truth system.
- Premium verification must mean stronger evidence, not purchased credibility.
- Managed legal services must be clearly separated from legal advice unless delivered by qualified counsel.
