# Web, Mobile, Information Architecture, and User Journeys

## Web-First Strategy

Web is correct for the first three phases because:

- public certificates must be linkable and indexable
- enterprise and agency work is document-heavy
- contracts, evidence, and policy editing require large screens
- reviewers need side-by-side media, scope, and audit context
- public verification should not require an app
- platform integrations depend on web APIs and URLs
- deployment and iteration are faster

The web product is not a temporary substitute for mobile. It is the permanent public registry and professional system of record.

## Phase Strategy

### Phase 1: Web Registry Platform

- public landing and education
- certificate lookup
- rights-holder and production records
- creator submission
- agency review
- policy and agreement workflows
- operator controls

### Phase 2: Professional Dashboards

- portfolio and roster operations
- authority and delegation management
- monitoring and disputes
- enterprise issuance
- API, webhooks, and reports
- counsel and compliance views

### Phase 3: Mobile Companion

Use mobile for:

- approval notifications
- secure identity confirmation
- quick review of low-risk changes
- approve, reject, or escalate
- incident and misuse alerts
- certificate scan or lookup

Do not attempt to place complex policy drafting, agreement negotiation, or evidence analysis primarily on mobile.

## Core Journeys

### Rights Holder Onboarding

1. Select person, company, estate, or property owner.
2. Verify identity.
3. Add rights assets.
4. Add representative and authority evidence.
5. Define public profile.
6. Create rights policies.
7. Invite reviewers.
8. Receive first request.

### Creator Approval

1. Verify organization.
2. Create production.
3. Select rights holder or submit discovery request.
4. Declare use and distribution.
5. Accept preliminary policy.
6. Upload final submission version and evidence.
7. Execute required agreement.
8. Receive decision or revisions.
9. Publish only after certificate issuance.
10. Verify domains, accounts, URLs, and content.

### Agency Review

1. Confirm delegated authority.
2. Triage by risk, value, and deadline.
3. Compare current submission with policy and prior version.
4. Review content, transcript, disclosures, and distribution.
5. Escalate required decisions to talent.
6. Record decision and conditions.
7. Validate certificate preview.
8. Monitor post-publication usage.

### Public Verification

1. Open certificate link, scan code, or search.
2. See status before any promotional detail.
3. See what the certificate does and does not prove.
4. Confirm rights holder, production, scope, term, content, and location.
5. Inspect event history and assurance level.
6. Report mismatch or misuse.

### Brand Placement

1. Production requests a named product or brand use.
2. Brand representative verifies context and co-participants.
3. Confidential guideline review occurs.
4. Brand approves exact content and distribution.
5. Placement certificate links to production and performer certificates.
6. Brand monitors campaign and archives the record.

## UX Rules

- Show status first.
- Use plain language before legal detail.
- Keep public and confidential evidence clearly separated.
- Never use one green check to represent multiple trust claims.
- Make mismatch states prominent.
- Show why a certificate changed.
- Allow users to compare certificate versions.
- Make report and dispute actions visible.
- Avoid marketplace patterns such as carts, ratings, and asset grids.

## Accessibility and Internationalization

- WCAG 2.2 AA target
- keyboard and screen-reader support
- localized legal and policy wording, not literal translation only
- explicit timezone on audit events
- locale-aware names, addresses, dates, and currencies
- support for right-to-left layouts before global enterprise expansion
- avoid color-only status indicators
