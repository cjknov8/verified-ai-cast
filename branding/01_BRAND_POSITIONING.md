# Brand Positioning

## Brand Objective

Make the company feel like an independent institution for rights evidence, not an AI content company.

## Positioning Statement

For rights holders, productions, brands, and platforms that need reliable proof of authorization, the company provides a public registry of verified approvals, scope, content, distribution, and status. Unlike generation tools or asset marketplaces, it is neutral infrastructure focused on rights, evidence, and continuing validity.

## Brand Pillars

### Authority

Precise rules, disciplined issuance, reliable status, and accountable governance.

### Legibility

Complex rights expressed in language that creators, professionals, and the public can understand.

### Neutrality

Independent from any single generation model, studio, platform, agency, or brand.

### Permanence

Durable records, visible history, and no quiet deletion of uncomfortable events.

### Restraint

No hype, spectacle, or claims beyond the evidence.

## Message Hierarchy

1. Verify official authorization.
2. See who approved what and whether it remains valid.
3. Bind approval to content, scope, and distribution.
4. Give rights holders control and creators credible proof.
5. Build an industry registry over time.

## Category Language

Use:

- rights registry
- authorization record
- verified approval
- rights usage certificate
- public verification
- provenance and evidence
- active, revoked, expired
- rights holder and representative

Avoid:

- AI marketplace
- face marketplace
- clone licensing
- digital human store
- minting
- token
- trustless
- instant verification
- immutable truth
- legally guaranteed

## Brand Architecture

Recommended:

- **Parent company**: a neutral rights and registry name
- **Verified AI Cast**: performer product
- **Verified Productions**: production record product
- **Verified Placements**: brand product
- **Registry**: public lookup surface
- **Trust API**: enterprise verification product

This endorsed architecture preserves Phase 1 recognition while allowing the company to outgrow "Cast."

## Audience Messages

### Rights Holders

Official use should be specific, visible, and revocable.

### Creators

Permission is stronger when partners and audiences can verify it.

### Agencies

Turn representation and approval into a controlled operating record.

### Brands

Distinguish intentional placement from imitation without sacrificing exclusivity.

### Platforms and Advertisers

Check current authorization before relying on a rights claim.

## Industry Perception Risks

### Why Actors May Distrust It

- fear of normalizing replacement
- fear that identity data or models will leak
- concern that agencies will approve too broadly
- suspicion that "verification" is pay-to-play
- weak revocation enforcement

Response:

- no raw model marketplace
- explicit scope
- performer thresholds and delegation
- visible status and revocation
- strong privacy commitments
- rights-holder governance

### Why Agencies May Resist

- loss of relationship control
- added workflow
- price transparency pressure
- liability for authority claims
- concern that the platform disintermediates them

Response:

- agency operating tools
- private policy and pricing
- agency-branded client service where appropriate
- clear authority boundaries
- no direct bypass of active representation

### Why Creators May Ignore It

- no distributor requires it
- review adds time and cost
- unclear marketing benefit

Response:

- fast, predictable review
- public proof
- advertiser and platform acceptance
- reusable production identity
- reduced repeated diligence

### Why Brands May Reject It

- mass-market association
- visible AI framing
- loss of secrecy
- unproven authority

Response:

- selective private programs
- brand-first language
- delayed publication
- institutional governance
- premium service and archival presentation

## Proof, Not Promotion

The strongest brand marketing is the registry working correctly:

- a clear active record
- a clear mismatch warning
- a transparent revocation
- a well-run dispute
- a platform that relies on the result

Campaign copy should never outpace operational truth.
