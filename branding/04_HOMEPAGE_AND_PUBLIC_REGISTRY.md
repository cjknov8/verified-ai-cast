# Homepage and Public Registry Architecture

## Homepage Job

The homepage should establish category, explain the verification model, show a live registry object, and route each professional audience without looking like an asset marketplace.

## Navigation

```text
Brand
Registry
How It Works
Rights Holders
Productions
Agencies
Brands
Standards
Resources
Sign In
```

Primary CTA: `Verify a certificate`  
Secondary CTA: `Register as a rights holder`

Creator submission should remain available but should not be the only prominent action.

## Homepage Wireframe

### 1. Hero

Eyebrow:

`The public registry for authorized digital use`

Headline:

`Know what was officially approved.`

Body:

`Verify the rights holder, approval scope, content, distribution, and current certificate status for digital entertainment.`

Actions:

- Verify a certificate
- Explore how certification works

Visual:

A real certificate or registry lookup interface, not a generated performer portrait.

### 2. Source-of-Truth Statement

Headline:

`Generation is abundant. Authorization is specific.`

Explain that the platform does not prevent copying and does not generate the content. It records official authorization.

### 3. Trust Framework

Four columns:

- Verified identity
- Verified authority
- Recorded approval
- Content and location match

Each column states what it proves and what it does not prove.

### 4. Registry Demonstration

Show:

- active certificate
- revoked certificate
- URL mismatch
- content mismatch

This is more credible than logos or abstract trust claims.

### 5. Certificate Explanation

Diagram:

```text
Rights Holder
  -> Authorized Representative
  -> Approval Record
  -> Certificate
  -> Published Content and URL
  -> Ongoing Status
```

### 6. Rights Holder Section

`Define official use without selling control of your identity or property.`

Features:

- policies
- review
- approval
- revocation
- monitoring
- history

### 7. Creator and Production Section

`Turn permission into proof.`

Features:

- project record
- structured submission
- multi-party approvals
- public verification
- reusable production profile

### 8. Agency Section

`Operate representation and approvals with evidence.`

Features:

- roster authority
- delegation
- policy
- review queues
- settlement
- audit

### 9. Brand Section

`Certify intentional placement without making the brand widely available.`

Emphasize selective programs, context, product integrity, and private workflows.

### 10. Industry Adoption

Do not use customer logos until authorized. Show the relying-party model:

- agencies issue approvals
- productions publish certificate IDs
- advertisers verify
- platforms check status
- audiences inspect public records

### 11. Standards and Governance

Link to:

- certificate policy
- assurance levels
- revocation policy
- technical schema
- transparency report
- advisory governance

### 12. FAQ

Required questions:

- Does a certificate stop copying?
- Does it prove copyright ownership?
- What happens after revocation?
- Can a certificate be copied to another site?
- How is content matched?
- Who can approve?
- Is the platform a marketplace?
- Does it work with any AI generation tool?
- Is public verification free?

### 13. CTA

Primary:

`Verify a record`

Professional:

`Apply for rights-holder onboarding`

## Conversion Strategy

Do not lead the institutional homepage with price.

Sequence:

1. Understand the verification claim.
2. Inspect a real registry record.
3. Choose participant role.
4. See process and evidence requirements.
5. Request onboarding or submit a project.
6. Receive a scoped quote.

## SEO and Public Authority

Build durable public resources:

- certificate definitions
- digital replica consent guide
- rights-holder authority guide
- status and revocation guide
- C2PA versus legal authorization explainer
- jurisdiction research updates
- transparency reports

These resources should be written as professional reference material, not AI trend content.
