# Design System

## Design Objective

Communicate institutional trust, premium restraint, and evidentiary clarity.

The visual reference set is closer to professional information services, legal institutions, financial infrastructure, and luxury editorial design than to AI software.

## Principles

- Evidence over decoration
- Status over spectacle
- Editorial hierarchy over dashboard clutter
- Quiet confidence over hype
- Consistency over novelty
- Accessible contrast over subtlety that hides meaning

## Color

### Core

- Paper: `#F7F5F0`
- White: `#FFFFFF`
- Ink: `#111615`
- Navy: `#14202B`
- Slate: `#44515A`
- Line: `#D8D5CE`

### Institutional Accent

- Deep blue: `#173B57`
- Muted gold: `#A8844F`

Gold should indicate institution or premium service, not status validity.

### Status

- Active: deep green with text and icon
- Suspended: amber
- Revoked: deep red
- Expired: neutral gray
- Superseded: blue-gray
- Unresolved: amber or violet-gray, not generative-AI purple

Every status uses a label and icon, never color alone.

## Typography

### Sans

Use a highly legible professional grotesk for UI, records, and body copy.

### Serif

Use an editorial serif for public headlines, reports, and archival presentation.

### Mono

Use for certificate IDs, hashes, timestamps, API fields, and evidence references.

Korean typography should be selected as a first-class system, not a fallback. Maintain comparable visual authority without forcing Latin-style letter spacing.

## Logo Direction

Avoid:

- sparkle or neural-network symbols
- face outlines
- shields with check marks
- blockchain cubes
- play buttons
- initials that lock the identity to AI Cast

Explore:

- registry seal built from lines or entries
- typographic wordmark
- restrained issuer mark
- monogram based on the future parent name
- subtle registration or annotation motif

The mark must work at certificate favicon size and in black-and-white legal documents.

## Certificate Design

Information order:

1. Status
2. Certificate type and ID
3. Rights holder and approving authority
4. Approved production and use
5. Content match
6. Distribution match
7. Term and territory
8. Assurance level
9. History and evidence
10. Report issue

Do not make certificates resemble awards. They are verification records.

## Public Registry Design

- white or paper background for most records
- dark institutional header
- visible status rail
- structured metadata
- expandable technical evidence
- event timeline
- clear mismatch notices
- print and PDF-friendly layout

## Professional Workspace

- dense but calm
- persistent entity and authority context
- side-by-side submission and policy
- visible delegation and signer identity
- high-risk actions require explicit confirmation
- avoid large promotional cards inside operational screens

## Imagery

Use sparingly:

- documentary production details
- archival records
- controlled studio environments
- materials and craft for brand programs

Avoid AI-generated portraits as primary identity. They weaken the claim that the company is about real authorization.

## Motion

- minimal
- functional transitions
- no glowing scans, generative particle fields, or rotating ledgers
- respect reduced motion
- status changes should be clear and immediate

## Tone

Use:

- "This certificate is active."
- "This URL is not within the approved scope."
- "Authority was verified through the represented agency until..."

Avoid:

- "Trust the future."
- "Revolutionizing identity."
- "100% authentic."
- "Immutable proof."
- "AI, verified."

## Current MVP Evolution

Keep:

- dark green-black institutional surfaces
- cream editorial sections
- restrained gold
- serif public headlines
- clear certificate presentation

Change over time:

- reduce cinematic talent imagery
- move from `VA` to a scalable parent mark
- increase white-space and document-like registry layouts
- make assurance and evidence more visible
- remove fixed-price emphasis from the public institutional homepage
