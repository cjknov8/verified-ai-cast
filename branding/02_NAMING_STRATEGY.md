# Naming Strategy

## Current Name: Verified AI Cast

### Pros

- Immediately communicates performers and AI
- Works for the Phase 1 wedge
- "Verified" reinforces approval and trust
- Understandable to creators and agencies
- Existing product and visual equity

### Cons

- "Cast" excludes brands, characters, products, and broader rights
- "AI" can reduce luxury compatibility and institutional neutrality
- Sounds like a casting or talent marketplace
- "Verified" is widely used and difficult to own
- The acronym `VAC` is not desirable as a global authority mark
- May feel tactical to enterprise and investors

### Scalability

Strong as a performer product. Weak as the permanent parent company or industry-wide registry.

### Global Perception

English meaning is clear, but "cast" is entertainment-specific. Translation may shift toward casting services rather than rights verification.

### Enterprise Credibility

Moderate. It can sell a focused workflow but does not naturally carry standards, registry, or infrastructure authority.

### Trademark Risk

The phrase combines descriptive terms and may be difficult to protect broadly. A formal clearance search is required in every intended class and jurisdiction.

### Luxury Compatibility

Moderate to low. The explicit "AI" label and casting connotation may conflict with discretion and heritage.

### Investor Appeal

Good wedge clarity, limited platform breadth.

## Naming Criteria

Score each from 1-5:

- `Clarity`: communicates rights or verification
- `Scale`: works beyond performers
- `Authority`: sounds institutional
- `Luxury`: supports premium programs
- `Distinctiveness`: potentially ownable
- `Global`: pronounceable and transferable

Trademark, corporate-name, domain, and linguistic clearance are not included in these scores.

## Recommendations by Use

### Best Enterprise Name

**Verity Registry**

Reason: serious, cross-category, and compatible with registry and API products. Risk: "Verity" is used widely and may be difficult to clear.

### Best Luxury Name

**Provenance House**

Reason: editorial, discreet, and compatible with heritage and archival positioning. Risk: less direct for infrastructure and may sound like a consultancy.

### Best Consumer Name

**Official Use**

Reason: plain-language promise that maps to public verification. Risk: highly descriptive and weak as a protectable company name.

### Best Long-Term Infrastructure Name

**RightsMark**

Reason: connects rights with a visible certification mark and works across certificates, APIs, and registry records. Risk: likely crowded naming territory and requires careful trademark clearance.

### Best Descriptive Category Name

**Verified Rights Registry**

Reason: use as the category and product descriptor even if it is not the protectable parent name.

## Naming Process

1. Shortlist 5-7 names from distinct linguistic directions.
2. Conduct knockout searches for companies, products, domains, apps, and social handles.
3. Run counsel-led trademark clearance in priority classes and countries.
4. Test pronunciation and negative meaning in English, Korean, Japanese, French, German, Spanish, and Chinese.
5. Test with actors, agencies, brands, studio counsel, and investors.
6. Check whether the name can support an issuer mark and certificate ID.
7. Keep Verified AI Cast until the parent brand can be launched with real registry capability.

## Naming Warning

Avoid adopting "Authority" in a way that implies government status, and avoid "Registry" claims that imply title adjudication unless the service clearly describes its private and evidentiary nature.
